Emergencies rarely start when everyone is at home. The children are at school, someone is at work, and the phones do not connect. A family plan is a handful of decisions made in advance, so nobody has to invent them under stress. You can put one together in an evening.

## 1. Write down the contacts, on paper

Phones run flat and get lost. On a card in every wallet and every [go-bag](/en/guides/go-bag-checklist/), write:

- every household member's number,
- an out-of-town contact: one person everyone calls or texts when you cannot reach each other,
- school, nursery, workplaces, your doctor,
- the emergency number (112 in Europe, 911 in North America).

Give younger children a card with a caregiver's contact details, as the Polish government guide recommends.

## 2. Agree on meeting places

| Level | Where | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| By the house | a street corner, a playground | sudden fire, leaving the building |
| In the neighbourhood | a school, a friend's house | you cannot get back home |
| Out of town | relatives or friends elsewhere | the whole area is evacuated |

Agree on a check-in rule too, such as: if there is no contact, everyone checks messages on the hour.

## 3. Split the jobs

Who grabs the bags, who takes documents and medication, who handles the pets, who collects the children. One sentence per person is enough. Plan specifically for anyone who needs help: older relatives, people with disabilities, small children.

## 4. Plan your routes

- Two ways out of your home.
- Two routes out of town in case one road is blocked.
- A printed local map in the go-bag: navigation apps may not work.
- No car? Know where the nearest official assembly point is and how you would get there on foot.

## 5. When the authorities order an evacuation

Following the Polish government's Safety Guide:

1. Close the windows, turn off the water, switch off electrical and gas appliances.
2. Dress for the weather.
3. Take your go-bag.
4. Check whether neighbours need help.
5. Use the transport the authorities organise, or walk to the designated area.
6. Tell your family which way you are going and where.
7. Never hand your documents to strangers offering help.

Follow official instructions on the radio, TV and your national alert app.

## 6. Communication when networks are overloaded

- Text, do not call. The FCC and FEMA advise limiting non-emergency calls to keep networks free and save battery.
- Wait about 10 seconds before redialling a call that does not connect.
- Send one message to your out-of-town contact: "I'm at X, I'm OK, heading to Y."
- Turn on battery saver.

## 7. Practise once

A plan nobody has practised disappears under stress. Once a year, do a drill: everyone out of the house with their bags in five minutes, then walk to the first meeting place. Check the bags while you are at it.
