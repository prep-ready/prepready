A blackout is a large, long power cut that takes out a district, a city or a whole country. On 28 April 2025, almost all of Spain and Portugal lost power and restoration ran into the next day. Trains, metros, traffic lights and card payments all stopped.

The Polish government's Safety Guide warns that a power cut "may last several days". Without power, heating, internet and mobile networks can fail, and in many buildings so can the water.

## The short version

- Water: 1 gallon (3.8 l) per person per day, at least 3 days.
- Light: torches and headlamps with spare batteries, no candles near children or curtains.
- Communication: a battery or hand-crank radio and a charged power bank.
- Food: ready-to-eat, or a camping stove.
- Cash: card terminals and ATMs stop working.

## Blackout supplies checklist

| Item | Why | How much |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bottled water | drinking, cooking, hygiene | 1 gal / 3.8 l per person per day × 3 days |
| Torch or headlamp | light without fire risk | 1 per person + 2 sets of batteries |
| Battery or crank radio | official updates without internet | 1 per household |
| Power bank | phone, USB light | fully charged |
| No-cook food | tins, crackers, nuts, bars | 3 days |
| Camping stove + gas | a hot meal, boiling water | window open only |
| Cash | when cards fail | small notes |
| Warm layers, blankets, sleeping bags | winter heat loss | for everyone |
| Medication | continuity of care | at least a week |
| First aid kit | minor injuries | 1 per household |

For how to store and rotate water and food, see our [water and food supply guide](/en/guides/emergency-water-and-food-supply/).

## Risks specific to flats

- Water. Upper floors often rely on electric pumps. Fill the bath and pots as soon as the lights go out.
- Lifts. Avoid them during storms and rolling outages. Plan how older neighbours on high floors will use the stairs.
- Heating. Central heating depends on electric pumps. In winter a flat cools within hours.
- Entry phones and garage doors stop working. Carry a physical key.
- Fridge and freezer. Keep them shut; a closed freezer stays frozen for many hours.

## The first hour

1. Check whether it is just you (the fuse box) or the whole building and street.
2. Unplug electronics such as the computer, TV and washing machine, as the Polish guide advises, because the power surge on restoration can damage them. Leave one lamp switched on so you know when power returns.
3. Fill the bath, pots and bottles while there is still pressure.
4. Turn on the radio and follow official updates.
5. Switch your phone to battery saver. Text rather than call.
6. Call emergency services only if life or health is at risk. Unnecessary calls block the lines.

## If it lasts more than a day

- Heat. Gather everyone in one room, ideally a sunny one. Cover the windows at night, dress in layers, sleep in sleeping bags.
- Food. Eat from the fridge first, then the freezer, then your stored supplies.
- Cooking. Use a camping stove only with a window open. Never heat a room with a gas cooker, barbecue or generator: carbon monoxide has no smell and it kills.
- Neighbours. Check on older neighbours: water, medication, warmth.

## Generator, power station or power bank?

In a flat a fuel generator is out: never run one indoors, on a balcony or in a stairwell. Power banks for phones and USB lights, or a portable power station for bigger needs, are the sensible choice. In a house, a generator can make sense, but only outdoors and with stored fuel.

## Before the lights go out

Thirty minutes today does most of the work: test torches and batteries, charge a power bank, put some cash aside and buy a pack of water. The rest you can add over time. If you rent, our [guide for renters](/en/guides/emergency-preparedness-for-renters/) covers small-space storage.
